# Design research notes (Oct 13, 2026)

Sources checked with WebFetch: americanheritage.org and pinecrest.edu.

- **americanheritage.org** turned out to be the *American Heritage Education Foundation* (a nonprofit that publishes lesson plans), not American Heritage School in FL (that's ahschool.com). Takeaways anyway: resources are grouped by grade band (Elementary / Middle / High), the mission statement comes first, and "Who it's for" (teachers, homeschoolers, students) is stated plainly.
- **pinecrest.edu** (PK-12, Fort Lauderdale/Boca Raton): a short, confident hero line ("See every Perspective") with a one-line subhead saying who it serves; lots of whitespace; repeated sections built as a short serif/sans heading plus a two-line description; real photos of real spaces instead of stock art; a calm, restrained palette.
- Cues used here: a short premium headline plus one clear subhead; content organized by grade band and skill; real-product imagery (our sample worksheet layouts) in place of cartoons; trust built from transparency (open frameworks, clear level definitions) rather than badges; serif-free but warm type (Nunito headings / Inter body); navy/cream base with teal CTAs.
- Neither site's content, logos, or claims are reused. No endorsement is implied.
