Our educational approach

Designed around established learning standards and research-informed teaching practices.

You can help your child. We use established learning standards to choose the skills, and research-informed teaching practices to organize the practice.

  • Common Core learning standards. The learning goals practiced.
  • IES guidance. Building skills progressively, and connecting objects, pictures, and equations.
  • Structured literacy. Explicitly connecting sounds, letters, reading, and spelling.
  • NAEYC. Adapting practice to the child, and hands-on learning.

Learning ideas behind the design

These research-informed ideas shape how the pages are organized.

  • Zone of proximal developmentNew work should sit just past what a child can do alone, where a little help makes it possible. Scaffolding and the zone of proximal development (ERIC)
  • ScaffoldingAn adult helps with the hard part, then takes that help away as the child can do more. Wood, Bruner, and Ross described this in 1976. Scaffolding and the zone of proximal development, revisited (ERIC)
  • A progression from objects to symbolsTeach number ideas in order. Begin with objects and informal marks, then connect them to number symbols. IES practice guide: Teaching Math to Young Children
  • Progress monitoringWatch what a child can do, then choose a next step that is not too easy and not too hard. IES practice guide: Teaching Math to Young Children
  • Developmentally appropriate practiceBuild on what this child can already do, in a way that fits that child. NAEYC: defining developmentally appropriate practice

Sounds, letters, reading, and spelling

A child learns that a letter stands for a sound. The letter m stands for the sound at the beginning of map. They blend sounds to read a word, and they spell the pattern they just learned.

CVC words, in plain English

CVC words are simple words like cat, map, and sit. You can hear three sounds: a consonant, a short vowel, and another consonant. A longer explanation is in our parent guide to CVC words.

High-frequency words and sight words

A high-frequency word is a word that occurs often in text, such as the, and, or said.

A sight word is a word a reader recognizes automatically, instantly. A high-frequency word can become a sight word once your child knows it right away. They are not the same thing.

Practice will connect the sounds and the spelling, including any part that does not follow the usual pattern. Recognizing the word instantly comes from that connection.
